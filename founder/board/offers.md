# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **One sharp, painful use case exists.** CBSE exam-centre control (datesheet import from CBSE PDFs, seating, invigilator duties, Form 66, answer-sheet dispatch) solves a deadline-driven, high-stakes problem, where a mistake has real consequences for the school. Under the Offers lens this is the best part of the business: the outcome is concrete and urgent.
- **The buyer is defined and can be found.** Private CBSE schools with 300–2,000 students, sold to the principal or owner, starting in Haryana and North India. The segment is narrow enough to reach on purpose.
- **Low effort to start.** Self-serve signup, a 14-day trial with every module open, UPI autopay, GST invoices and free logins for parents and staff all reduce the effort and risk of trying it.
- **The modules are a natural stack.** Fees, payroll, timetable, calendar and family app each answer a separate problem the buyer runs into. The pieces of a strong bundle are already there.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No proof, so low perceived likelihood.** The number of paying schools is unknown, and some existing schools are "grandfathered" in for free. An owner moving the school's records and fees onto software from a one-person company needs evidence that it works and will still be there next year. Today the offer gives none: no case studies, no guarantee, no published support commitment.
2. **It sells price and features, not an outcome.** The offer is "14 modules, à la carte, ₹60,000/yr, 50% off." That puts it next to every other school ERP, compared on price. LAUNCH50 cuts price when it should add value, and it trains buyers to expect discounts.
3. **The effort of switching falls on the buyer.** Getting results from an ERP means importing students, fee structures, staff and payroll. Nothing in the offer says who does that work. With one developer and unknown sales and support capacity, either the school does the work (and drops off) or the founder does (and it does not scale). Seven of the 14 modules are still partial or thin, which weakens any claim to cover the "complete school."

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many schools pay today, at what average price, and how many renewed?
- Of the 14-day trials, how many convert, and at what step do schools drop off?
- Which module do paying schools buy first? Is it the exam-centre module?
- Who migrates a new school's data, and how long does it take?
- What does one school cost to win and to support each year? (CAC and support cost are open.)
- What result could you guarantee within 30 days, and what would you refund if you missed it?
- Could you charge double for the exam-centre outcome alone and still win?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Lead with one outcome.** Make the exam-centre module the front offer, built around the board-exam deadline: "run your CBSE centre with zero errors this season." Urgency here is real, not invented.
2. **Stack done-for-you onboarding** (data import, staff training, exam-week support) and attach a named, specific guarantee. Drop the half-price launch discount.
3. **Show proof within two quarters:** a named set of paying, renewing schools with measured results, such as hours saved and errors avoided.
4. **Stop calling it "complete school"** until the partial modules are finished, or sell only the mature modules until then.
