# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **There is a small market it could own.** CBSE examination-centre control (datesheet import from CBSE PDFs, seating, invigilator duties, Form 66, answer-sheet dispatch) is a narrow, painful, recurring job that a general school ERP does not usually treat as its core. That is the kind of small market the lens looks for: few buyers, sharp pain, and a chance of taking a big share of it.
- **It has the start of proprietary know-how.** Parsing CBSE's own documents and modelling centre rules is specific and slow to copy well. It is not a 10x breakthrough yet, but it is the one place where it might become one.
- **Switching costs build up.** Once fees, payroll, timetables and student records live in one shared database, leaving gets expensive. That gives some durability later, though it is lock-in, not a moat in its own right.
- **Cheap to deliver.** Self-serve signup, Razorpay autopay and one codebase keep the cost of each extra school low.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No distribution.** One developer-founder, unknown sales capacity, unknown CAC. K-12 ERP is usually sold in person to owners and principals, with implementation and data migration on top. A good product that cannot reach 100 schools is not a business.
2. **It is sold as a crowded, me-too product.** "14 modules for a whole school" is a comparison with every incumbent school ERP the buyer already knows, so it competes on features and price. The low price (₹60,000/yr, half off in year one) shows a business fighting on price, not one setting it. How much of the market incumbents hold in the region is unknown.
3. **It can be copied, and it is spread too thin.** Apart from the exam-centre module, nothing here is 10x better. An incumbent could build a CBSE-centre feature within months. Meanwhile, 14 modules (several thin or partial) split one founder's time across a wide front instead of building one lead that is hard to catch.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many schools use it today, and how many pay? How did each one arrive, and what did each cost to win?
- How many CBSE examination centres are there in Haryana and North India, and what share do you serve? (Unknown: this is the small market to size.)
- Who else sells exam-centre software to these schools, and what do centres use today: spreadsheets, an incumbent ERP, or nothing?
- What do you know about CBSE schools that a well-funded ERP company does not?
- Why now? What has changed in CBSE rules, digital payments or school buying behaviour?
- What stops an incumbent from copying the exam-centre module in month six?
- How long is your runway, and who will sell and implement while you build?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Lead with exam-centre control as the wedge. Sell it as the product, and treat the other modules as expansion once a school is in.
2. Show the share of a defined small market (for example, X of Y CBSE centres in one region), with the real count of paying schools.
3. Name a repeatable channel for the first 100 schools (CBSE centre networks, school trusts, a partner) with a measured cost per school.
4. Freeze new modules until the thin and partial ones are finished, or cut them.
