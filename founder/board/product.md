# Product lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A sharp wedge already exists.** CBSE exam-centre control is mature and very specific: it imports CBSE PDFs, makes seating plans and invigilator duties, handles Form 66 and answer-sheet dispatch. That one job is painful and happens on fixed dates, and few generic ERPs do it well. A product people want to start using begins with a job like this.
- **Care in the parts nobody sees.** Encrypted nightly backups, workspace archives, GST invoices, Razorpay autopay, deny-by-default access control and branded email templates. Each one is invisible until it fails, and building them signals taste.
- **Much of the experience is owned end to end.** Self-serve signup, a 14-day trial with everything open, in-app payment, free family logins, and a mobile app with push. The buyer is not handed off to resellers or spreadsheets.
- **Simple price shape.** Three price tiers, one bundle, and a student cap that is easy to understand.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus.** There are 14 modules, but only about 6 are mature and 3 are thin. The "complete school" bundle and the free trial show every module at once, including the thin ones. A principal who opens Library or Medical and finds one small screen will judge the whole product by its weakest page. The pitch "AWS for schools" describes the architecture, not what the customer experiences.
2. **One builder spread across 14 surfaces.** Craft cannot keep pace across this much product. Onboarding, data migration, training and support are where school software usually breaks, and capacity for them is unknown. Any seam there is part of the product.
3. **The first five minutes are undefined.** For a school, the real first experience is loading its students, staff and fee data. The brief does not say what that feels like, how long it takes, or who does it. If the school has to do it alone, the trial dies before it shows any value.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence: what is the one thing a school buys Capabble for?
- Walk me through a principal's first 30 minutes in the trial. What do they see, and what do they have to type?
- How many schools use it today, and which modules do they actually open every week?
- Who moves a school's existing data in, and how long does that take?
- Which modules would you hide or remove from the price page tomorrow if you had to?
- What happened with the schools on the "grandfather" path: why are they free, and would they pay?
- When a school breaks something during exam week, who picks up the phone?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Lead with one or two mature modules.** Pick exam-centre control and/or finance and make that the front door. Hide or label as "coming soon" every module that is thin or partial until it meets the same bar.
2. **Design onboarding first.** Script and measure the first-session journey, data import included. Then report time-to-first-value for real schools.
3. **Show usage proof.** Bring numbers on paying schools and weekly active use per module before any money goes into building more modules.
4. **Have a named plan for support and implementation.** Say who does this beyond the founder, before scaling sales.
